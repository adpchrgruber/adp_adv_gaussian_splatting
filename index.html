04 One splat

Click the photo or the splats to pick the splat that paints that spot.

Here a splat is 9 numbers. A 3D splat has 59: position 3, scale 3, rotation 4, opacity 1 and 48 colour coefficients, so its colour can change with the viewing angle.

05 How it works

1 Photos. Take many photos around a scene. Structure from motion finds where each camera stood and a sparse point cloud of features.

2 Splats. Every point becomes a 3D Gaussian: a soft, coloured, semi-transparent ellipsoid with a position, size, rotation, colour and opacity.

3 Render. For one camera, each ellipsoid is projected into an ellipse. The ellipses are sorted by depth and blended front to back until a pixel is full. This page does exactly this step, in 2D.

4 Compare. The render is compared with the real photo. The error flows back to every splat that touched a pixel (the gradient): move, stretch, turn, recolour, fade.

5 Densify. Where splats keep being pulled, they are split or cloned. Faint ones are removed.

6 Repeat thousands of times, with a different photo each step. A splat must look right from every camera, so it ends up at the right depth. The result renders in real time.
